\section*{The story on one page}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{The story on one page}

\paragraph{The general claim.} Soil carbon carries a long history. Its slowest pools adjust to a change in
their input over centuries, much longer than the few decades a unit of carbon spends in the soil on
average, so the soil of today still reflects the land use and the inputs of long before any model starts.
The convention is to start soil models in balance with their present litter, which assumes that history
away. \textbf{And the assumption decides the forecast}: a soil assumed balanced has no room left to gain, so
its projected sink depends only on future changes in litter and climate, while a soil still catching up
with its past keeps a sink of its own.

\paragraph{The connecting object: a trilemma.} Any model of a soil's trajectory has to reconcile three
sources of information: the measured \emph{soil series}, the \emph{kinetics} (decomposition rates and
climate response, themselves the product of earlier calibrations against measurements), and the measured
\emph{inputs}. A model started in balance can satisfy two of them at most. The threads of the paper meet
there: the trade-off between input and turnover, the missing input, the history of each plot, and the
forecast.

\paragraph{The test field.} Finland is where all three are measured on the same ground: a landscape
reshaped by a century of land use and regrowth, three soil campaigns on the same plots over four decades,
a plot-level litter record checked against the national inventory, and published kinetics for the
inventory's own model family. On the same models and the same data, a balanced start cannot reproduce
the measured accumulation, a start estimated from the measurements brackets it, and most of the modelled
sink of 1985--2024 is then the soil still catching up with its own history. Plot by plot, the same
history is what the models miss.

\paragraph{The consequences.} Relaxing the assumption changes what the models say about the future of the
Finnish forest soil sink: with the history the soil keeps room to gain, about a tenth more litter input would
hold the sink of the past for twenty years, and warming takes part of it back --- a direct input to the
debate on the national greenhouse gas balance, which a soil assumed balanced cannot inform.

\section{Introduction}
\arch{1}{\archIntro}

\paragraph{1. Soil carbon carries a long history, and its change is usually modelled.} Inventories report
the annual change in the soil carbon stock, not the stock. A few countries measure it (Sweden, Germany);
most model it, Finland with Yasso07. The slow pools adjust over centuries, while the mean transit time is
decades (Sierra et al.\ 2018): a soil keeps the memory of its past inputs for far longer than its
turnover suggests.

\paragraph{2. The convention assumes that history away.} Models are started in balance with a constant
litter and climate --- the Finnish inventory, the nationwide Yasso07 evaluation, RothC, the land models ---
and the inventory justifies it in one sentence (``approximately ten years of simulation since spin-up is
enough to cancel out the effect of the spin-up level''). A balanced start responds to every change after
its start date, but it cannot carry a deficit that already existed on that date: the site's history is
replaced by an equilibrium with its present litter.

\paragraph{3. The assumption decides the forecast.} A soil assumed balanced has no room left to gain: its
projected sink comes only from future changes in litter and climate. The reported Finnish soil term shows
what that means in practice: a sink for most of the reporting period, a source from 2021 once the litter
input was recalculated --- in a model started in balance, the soil term follows the litter estimate.
\begin{warn}
\textbf{Tone.} Describe the method, never judge the inventory (Aleksi leads it and is a coauthor). The safe
sentence: \emph{the reported soil term is, in both directions, the response of a modelled soil to a litter
input that departs from the level the soil was started in balance with.}
\end{warn}

\paragraph{4. The trilemma --- where the threads meet.} A model of a soil's trajectory has three sources of
information to reconcile: the \emph{soil series} (repeated measurements of the stock), the \emph{kinetics}
(decomposition rates and climate response, calibrated against measurements in earlier work), and the
\emph{inputs} (the litter, measured or derived plot by plot). In a soil in balance, the stock is the input
times the transit time, so the three are tied together: given the inputs and the kinetics, the stock and
its change follow, and a soil that is gaining carbon while its litter has stopped rising cannot be in
balance with that litter. A balanced start can therefore satisfy two of the three at most. The paragraph
should be short; the triangle carries the argument, and the threads it connects are named once here and
come back later: the trade-off between input and turnover (R2), the missing input (R2, Discussion), the
history of each plot (R4), and the forecast (R3). \textbf{The trilemma rests on the runs without the
transient start}; they cannot appear in the Introduction, so the trilemma is stated there as an
expectation and contextualised as soon as the Results begin (R1, F16).
\archTrilemma

\paragraph{5. Finland, the test field.} Where the general statement becomes measurable. A managed landscape
far from balance: slash-and-burn, grazing and high-grading, the war decades, then a century of regrowth
($+84\%$ growing stock) (Liski et al.\ 2006; Peltoniemi et al.\ 2004, 2007 said this in the group's own
words). Three soil campaigns on the same plots: $64.5 \to 71.5 \to 73.6$~tC\,ha$^{-1}$, gaining and then
slowing. A litter record for every plot (Tupek et al.), rising by about $45\%$ to 2006 and flat after,
which on the scope it shares with the national inventory agrees with it where most plots lie. And published
kinetics for the inventory's own model family. All three corners of the trilemma are measured on the same
ground.
\FIGQ{F10b_forest_history} \FIGQ{F3_mean_SOCalt}

\paragraph{6. Objectives --- the test, and its consequences.} Two revisions of the starting state: estimated
rather than assumed, and dated back to 1917, as far as the forest record allows. Each is tested against the
same models started in balance at the beginning of the measurements and recalibrated on the same data.
\textbf{And the applied aim, declared as such:} once the assumption is relaxed, what changes in the
forecast, and what does that tell us about the future of the Finnish greenhouse gas balance --- whether the
forest soil can stay a sink, for how long, how it responds to warming, and how much more litter input it
would take to keep the sink of the past. Questions:
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep0pt
  \item[(i)] Can a model started in balance reconcile the soil series, the kinetics and the inputs, and how
    much of the modelled sink does the history carry when the start is estimated?
  \item[(ii)] Where does reconciling them place the models on the trade-off between litter input and
    transit time?
  \item[(iii)] Once the assumption is relaxed, what follows for the future of the soil sink and of the Finnish
    greenhouse gas balance: how much room the soil has left, how the sink responds to warming, and how much
    additional litter input would hold it?
  \item[(iv)] What do the differences between plots say about the history each plot carries?
\end{enumerate}
\begin{warn}
\textbf{Scope of the applied claim, stated where it is declared.} The projections describe the calibrated

projection holds the litter at 2024 and recycles the recent climate. The applied answer is therefore a
statement about direction and sensitivity --- room left, response to warming, input needed --- not a
forecast of the reported numbers. National units (Mt\,CO$_2$\,yr$^{-1}$) need a sourced area and Aleksi's
review.
\end{warn}

\subsubsection{What the soil did}
\paragraph{} $+0.40$~tC\,ha$^{-1}$\,yr$^{-1}$ over the first interval, $+0.12$ over the second, $+0.26$ over
the span (310 plots, unweighted, true sampling years). The second interval is the fragile one (its interval
includes zero); the span is robust.

\subsubsection{A balanced start cannot satisfy the three --- the trilemma visualised}
\paragraph{The first result, and the moment the trilemma of the Introduction becomes visible.} Started in
balance in 1985 and recalibrated, all six
models gain $-0.05$ to $+0.04$ over 1985--2024 against the observed $+0.26$. They rise with the litter to 2006
and give the rise back after it, and they start about $7$~tC\,ha$^{-1}$ above the first campaign ($\sim$72 against
$64.5$), because a soil in balance with the 1985 litter holds more carbon than the soil held. Over
1985--2006 only SP1 reaches the observed interval; over 2006--2024 all six lose carbon. F16 shows it against
the campaigns: the two starts differ mainly at the first campaign, and separate again in the projection.
\FIG{F16_eqinit_trajectories} \FIGA{F17_eqinit_rates}

\dparagraph{The trilemma, contextualised.} The text names it here: held to the measured litter and to
kinetics near the published ones, the balanced start gives up the soil series --- the corner of the triangle
the Introduction predicted. It is free to bend the kinetics and does
--- less input, faster and more temperature-sensitive decomposition (transit time $22$--$27 \to 18$--$25$~yr)
--- and still cannot produce the accumulation. How far the kinetics would have to move is the corner the
comparison leaves partly open.
\begin{warn}
\textbf{Do not judge the two starts by the fit.} The likelihood is dominated by the stock levels, which both
starts reach (log-likelihoods within $6$); the rates and the early stock discriminate. Say so in two
sentences before a reader concludes the starts are equivalent.
\end{warn}
\begin{test}
\textbf{Close the third corner?} A balanced start with the rate priors widened would show how far the
kinetics must move to fit both series; one factor, six jobs. Or state it as a limitation.
\end{test}

\subsubsection{A first round of conclusions --- and the justification of the central choice}
\paragraph{} Three things are settled before anything else is built. (1) The trilemma is real on these data:
with the measured litter and near-published kinetics, a soil assumed balanced in 1985 cannot reproduce the
measured accumulation. (2) The failure survives the freedom to compensate, so it is not a matter of tuning.
(3) What the balanced start lacks is a state that is not in balance on the start date. \textbf{That is the
justification for the central choice of the paper}: to relax the steady-state assumption and estimate the
starting state from the measurements, which is what R2 does.

\subsubsection{Nested: the uncertainty, as the balanced start sees it --- $\sinput$ only}
\paragraph{The same side arm as at the end of R2, with one parameter.} The balanced start carries one auxiliary
uncertainty parameter, $\sinput$, which absorbs the uncertainty of the input estimate --- the point where the
data discussion (the litter product against the national inventory, S18) enters the calibration. In the
balanced start it relaxes (Yasso15 $1.54 \to 1.32$, TP2 $1.72 \to 1.60$), while the kinetics strain.
\textbf{$\sinit$ is not absent: it is fixed at $1$ by the steady-state assumption} --- a value never questioned
and never tested, only assumed. Implementation details here: the same error model and priors as R2, the fit
equivalence of the two starts (the likelihood dominated by levels) and why the comparison is made on the
rates.

to the new assumption. Everything that follows (the forecast, the plot analyses) is built on it, once it is
justified; its ramifications are examined, but the choice is the centre.
\end{ann}

\subsubsection{The central choice}
\paragraph{From the general idea to its implementation.} The idea: the soil may enter the observations out of
balance. The implementation: the start is estimated from the measurements and dated back to 1917, through
one quantity, $\sinit$, the deficit the soil carries --- the parameter the balanced start had fixed at $1$.
The same data, priors and likelihood. What $\sinit$ means, and what it costs, is in the nested part at the
end of R2.

\subsubsection{The estimated start reconciles the soil series}
\paragraph{} All six reach the three campaign levels. Over 1985--2024 they span $+0.10$ to $+0.35$ against
the observed $+0.26$, four of six inside its interval (SP1 $+0.13$, Yasso20 $+0.10$ below). Over 1985--2006
all six reach the observed rise; over 2006--2024 only TP2 and TP3 reach the observed $+0.12$, Yasso07 is near
zero, and SP1, Yasso15 and Yasso20 lose carbon. \FIG{F3_mean_soc}

\dparagraph{Bracketing is informative because it is not automatic.} Matching the rate would mean little if
any calibration on the same data could do it. The balanced start, calibrated on the same measurements with
one degree of freedom fewer, fails in every model.

\subsubsection{And most of the modelled sink is the history}
\paragraph{The division of the sink.} Six-model average, 1985--2024: of $+10.5$~tC\,ha$^{-1}$ gained, the
deficit inherited from before 1985 carries $+9.3$; the litter change since 1985 adds $+8.6$; the warming
removes $-7.5$. \FIG{S16_attribution_ensemble_smooth10} \FIGA{S15_attribution_by_model_smooth10}

\dparagraph{Necessary, and dominant.} The balanced start shows that the history is \emph{necessary} --- with
everything else free, the models cannot do without it. The division shows how much it \emph{weighs} --- most
of the sink, while the litter rise and the warming largely offset each other. The second answers the
objection to the first (``the inherited part is large only because the calibration chose a low $\sinit$''):
the data demand the deficit.
\begin{warn}
\textbf{The numbers will move} with the 1961--1984 pre-run climate: the climate part will appear from 1985,
the inherited part may shrink. The robust statement is the ordering --- most of the sink inherited, litter
and warming largely offsetting each other.
\end{warn}
\begin{test}
\textbf{S16 in three parts or five terms} (the two joint effects of climate as hatched bands)?
\end{test}

value. \FIG{F12_mrt_yasso}

\subsubsection{Across the ridge --- the trilemma resolved as far as the data allow}
\paragraph{} The soil fixes the product of input and transit time, and the posteriors ride that trade-off.
The two starts land at different places on it: in five of the six models the balanced start sits at less
input and faster turnover (SP1, a single slow pool, barely moves). \FIG{F18_eqinit_tradeoff}
\FIGA{F14_mrt_ridge}

\dparagraph{The echo, in parameter space.} \textbf{The triangle comes back once more}, after F16 showed it
in the trajectories. The
soil series is the ridge; the measured input (the dotted line) and the published kinetics sit off it; a
balanced start can stand on at most two of the three, and the estimated start, carrying the history,
stands somewhere else on the same ridge. Relaxing the assumption moves the models across it, and the
trade-off translates according to the new assumption. The text should say it in one or two sentences ---
after all the work, the trilemma is visible --- without redrawing the figure.

\dparagraph{What the resolution leaves.} The tension is not removed but concentrated in one corner: the soil
series is met and the kinetics stay near the published values (Yasso20 on its own, Yasso07 and Yasso15
faster), while the input the models need stays above what the product supplies. That remaining gap is the
thread to the Discussion of the inputs the product may miss.

\subsubsection{Nested: the uncertainty, as the estimated start sees it --- $\sinput$ and $\sinit$}
\paragraph{The same side arm as at the end of R1, now with two parameters.} $\sinit$ becomes needed, and
\textbf{that need was there before}: in R1 it was hidden by forcing the steady-state assumption, which fixes it
at $1$. R2 may look more uncertain, but it only makes explicit a quantity R1 assumed without testing. The two
auxiliary parameters share a width by design.
\begin{itemize}\itemsep0pt
  \item \textbf{$\sinput$} --- more input than measured: effective flux $3.19$--$4.34$ against $2.511$, inside the
    envelope of boreal production, set by the likelihood rather than the prior (the posterior stays above the
    prior centre whichever anchor is used). The national inventory's full estimate is $1.08$--$1.26\times$ the
    product on the southern plots, bracketing the understorey-corrected anchor ($1.21$) and below the
    posteriors ($1.27$--$1.73$); SX sets it against independent estimates.
  \item \textbf{$\sinit$} --- the deficit the data demand: $0.49$--$0.60$ in five of six models, lower than the
    growing-stock record implies ($\sim 0.90$), so read as an \emph{effective starting deficit of the soil}
    (slash-and-burn, raking, heavy cutting) rather than a ratio of litter inputs. It carries the forecast:
    headroom correlates with it at $-0.79$ to $-0.97$ (R3).
  \item \textbf{The implementation, and its advantages}: the error model (region, plot and campaign offsets
    integrated out, a declared total of $0.800$), what it buys (an honest number of independent observations,
    $221$), and the Bayesian points --- priors as information, homogenised across models; the posterior
    against the prior.
\end{itemize}
\FIG{F9_auxiliary_sigmas} \FIGA{SX_input_vs_literature}
\begin{warn}
\textbf{The direction depends on the comparator, so the title names none.} Against the published parameters
the calibration has faster turnover (Yasso07, Yasso15); against the balanced start, slower. ``Displaces
along'' covers both; never add a direction.
\end{warn}

\subsection*{\textsc{R3 --- side arm: what it means for the forecast and the GHG balance}}
\addcontentsline{toc}{subsection}{R3 --- side arm: what it means for the forecast and the GHG balance}
\paragraph{The applied answer to objective (iii).} Built on the central choice of R2: what the relaxed
assumption says about the future of the soil sink --- room left, the input that would hold it, the response to
warming --- and so about the Finnish greenhouse gas balance, within the scope declared in the objectives.
\arch{5}{\archRthree}

\subsubsection{Headroom only with the history}
\paragraph{In prose; the figure is in the appendix.} Projected over 2025--2044 with the litter held at 2024
and the recent climate recycled, the balanced start gives a sink of $-0.06$ to $+0.02$~tC\,ha$^{-1}$\,yr$^{-1}$
and a headroom of $-3$ to $+11\%$; the estimated start gives $+0.07$ to $+0.22$ and $+26$ to $+42\%$ --- the
disequilibrium Liski et al.\ (2006) found by another route ($+38\%$). The general claim of the
Introduction, measured: the starting state decides whether the soil has room left. \FIGA{F19_eqinit_forecast}

\subsubsection{How much input would hold the sink?}
\paragraph{The question a ministry would ask.} The models are linear in their input, so the projections with
the litter held and with $+20\%$ answer it exactly for any target sink (\texttt{doublechecks/input\_to\_hold\_sink.R}):
\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{llccccc}
\hline
target sink & window & Yasso07 & Yasso15 & Yasso20 & TP2 / TP3 & SP1 \\
\hline
observed 1985--2024 ($+0.26$) & 10 yr & $+6\%$ & $+6\%$ & $+5\%$ & $+1$ / $+2\%$ & $+10\%$ \\
 & 20 yr & $\mathbf{+10\%}$ & $\mathbf{+9\%}$ & $\mathbf{+11\%}$ & $+3$ / $+4\%$ & $+12\%$ \\
each model's own 1985--2024 & 20 yr & $+11\%$ & $+10\%$ & $+3\%$ & $+11\%$ & $+6\%$ \\
observed 2006--2024 ($+0.12$) & 20 yr & $+1\%$ & $+1\%$ & $+3\%$ & $-8$ / $-7\%$ & $+5\%$ \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\dparagraph{About a tenth more input, sustained, holds the long-term sink for twenty years} ($0.3$--$0.5$~tC\,ha$^{-1}$\,yr$^{-1}$
of effective input in Yasso; each $+10\%$ buys $0.15$--$0.24$~tC\,ha$^{-1}$\,yr$^{-1}$ of sink). The gain lasts
only while the input does, and it postpones saturation rather than removing it.
\begin{warn}
\textbf{Lower bounds, and provisional:} no further warming is in these projections. The required input depends
on both the starting state (the baseline sink) and the transit time (the gain per unit of input) --- the
paper's two main results meet here.
\end{warn}
\begin{test}
\textbf{Which target}, the observed long-term rate, the recent one, or each model's own? A
warming-corrected column for Yasso? National units (Mt\,CO$_2$\,yr$^{-1}$) need a sourced area and Aleksi's eye.
\end{test}

\subsubsection{Warming, and whether it depends on where the calibration lands}
\paragraph{} Under warming to SSP2-4.5 the stock falls by $5$--$7$~tC\,ha$^{-1}$ by 2084 in all three Yasso
versions; only Yasso07 separates the published and calibrated parameters ($-2.08$~tC\,ha$^{-1}$), because its
single climate modifier ties turnover to temperature sensitivity. For the inventory, which runs Yasso07,
that is the applied point. \FIG{F15_forward_arms}

\section{Discussion --- how the models could do better}
\arch{7}{\par}

\paragraph{1. A starting state per plot.} The plot-level error fades at about the models' turnover pace: a
start built from each plot's own history (the last regeneration cut dated from the 1985 stand age) is the
natural next model step. The excess lives in stands young in 1985 (F11b). \FIGA{F11b_time_since_cut}

\paragraph{2. The inputs the litter product may miss.} Natural mortality --- now quantified in the national
inventory, $0.1$--$0.16$~tC\,ha$^{-1}$\,yr$^{-1}$ by now in the south; roots and rhizodeposition; understorey;
mycorrhizal fungi; fine-root lifespan (pointing the other way); early-succession vegetation. A set that
brackets the gap, never a sum that matches it.

\paragraph{3. Pioneer vegetation, where the two meet.} The excess carbon of the stands young in 1985 was built
during early stand development, either inherited or supplied by vegetation no tree-based product records;
the data do not separate the two. Lauri Mehtätalo (LUKE) to be cited \gapnote{which work}.

\paragraph{4. The site, briefly.} Fertility, texture and stones explain a further tenth of the residual
\gapnote{check whether the stone effect is partly the stone correction of the observed stocks}.

\section{Conclusions}
\arch{8}{\archConcl}

\paragraph{1.} The soil series, the kinetics and the inputs are reconciled only when the soil carries a
history: a balanced start cannot, an estimated start brackets the measured accumulation.
\paragraph{2.} That history carries most of the modelled sink of 1985--2024; the litter rise and the warming
largely offset each other.
\paragraph{3.} Reconciling the three moves the models along the trade-off between input and turnover, and asks
for more input than the litter product supplies.
\paragraph{4.} For the forecast and the Finnish greenhouse gas balance: the soil keeps room to gain only with
the history, finite and costly to realise; about a tenth more litter input would hold the past sink for twenty
years; warming takes part of it back, and for the inventory's own model the size of that response depends on
where the calibration lands.
\paragraph{5.} A baseline for further model development: a start per plot, and the inputs the product may
miss. (The national-inventory implications go just before the Limitations, not here.)
\paragraph{Bonus product.} The maps.
