\section{Physically-bounded litter-input priors}
\label{app:input-bounds}

\paragraph{Problem.} The input multiplier $\sigma_{\mathrm{input}}$ scales the
litter forcing every model integrates. Left as a weak, scale-free prior it lets
the likelihood drag two models (TP2, TP3) to effective litter fluxes of
$34$--$50$~tC\,ha$^{-1}$\,yr$^{-1}$ --- several times the net primary production of
any boreal forest. The multiplier's distance from unity is therefore a
\emph{structural-adequacy gauge}, interpretable only once the prior encodes a
physical ceiling.

\paragraph{Physical envelope.} The effective flux is
$\sigma_{\mathrm{input}}\times J$, with $J$ the shared tree-litter object (median
$\approx 2.8$~tC\,ha$^{-1}$\,yr$^{-1}$). We bound it to the boreal total-NPP
envelope of Gower et al.\ (2001), $[0.05,\,8.7]$~tC\,ha$^{-1}$\,yr$^{-1}$,
homogeneous across all six models and applied to \emph{both} ends of the pre-run:
the contemporary flux $F_{\mathrm{now}}=\sigma_{\mathrm{input}}J$ and the 1917
historical flux $F_{1917}=\sigma_{\mathrm{init}}\sigma_{\mathrm{input}}J$. The
ceiling ($8.7$, the Gower maximum) is the binding limit; the floor is relaxed
below the Gower minimum to a small, strictly-positive, non-binding guardrail ---
kept $>0$ so that zero flux (hence zero predicted SOC and a $-\infty$ likelihood)
cannot occur, and set low to pre-position for a future stratified $\sigma$.
Because $\sigma_{\mathrm{init}}$ has no independent physical referent --- it is a
\emph{ratio} of two fluxes --- it is not bounded on its own; instead $F_{1917}$ is
bounded to the same envelope and $\sigma_{\mathrm{init}}=F_{1917}/F_{\mathrm{now}}$
is recovered. The multipliers are scalar (network-average), so local disturbance
(clear-cuts) lives in the per-plot $J$, not in the bound.

\paragraph{Mechanism: bounded re-parameterisation.} A hard truncation would mix
poorly precisely where it bites, since the two binding models sit against the
ceiling. Instead we sample an unconstrained coordinate $z$ and map it through a
scaled logistic onto the window $[a,b]$,
\[
  p=g(z)=a+(b-a)\,\sigma(z),\qquad \sigma(z)=\tfrac{1}{1+e^{-z}},\qquad
  z=g^{-1}(p)=\log\tfrac{p-a}{\,b-p\,},
\]
so every proposal is feasible by construction and the boundary is approached
asymptotically, never struck. The change of variables contributes a Jacobian term
to the log-prior,
\[
  \log\!\left|g'(z)\right| = \log(p-a)+\log(b-p)-\log(b-a),
\]
which keeps the physical-space prior the one intended. The two fluxes are two such
coordinates; the multipliers are recovered as
$\sigma_{\mathrm{input}}=F_{\mathrm{now}}/\bar J$ and
$\sigma_{\mathrm{init}}=F_{1917}/F_{\mathrm{now}}$, with $\bar J$ a fixed
mean-litter units bridge. Implemented as one new transform type in the calibration
engine, homogeneous across the six prior specifications.

\paragraph{Consequence.} The escape hatch closes: misfit is forced onto the
(otherwise prior-pinned) kinetics or into a visible $R^2$ hit, exposing structural
inadequacy instead of hiding it in manufactured carbon.

\paragraph{Before/after diagnostic.} The main text reports only the final,
flux-bounded calibration. For completeness, we contrast it here
against the earlier \emph{unbounded} homogenized run (the calibration that motivated
the bound). Without the physical ceiling, TP2 and TP3 reach median effective fluxes
of $34$ and $50$~tC\,ha$^{-1}$\,yr$^{-1}$ --- 13--20$\times$ boreal NPP, physically
impossible --- while SP1, Yasso07/15/20 already sit inside the envelope. Under the
bound all six collapse into $[0.9,\,6.3]$, and, tellingly, \emph{only} the two
runaway models lose predictive skill: confirmation that they had been buying fit by
manufacturing carbon, not by resolving kinetics. The unbounded run is retained only
as this diagnostic (git-tagged, not a maintained parallel pipeline); it is not part
of the reported results.
